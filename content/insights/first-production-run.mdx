The jump from approved sample to the first carton is where quiet risk concentrates. Specs that felt finished on the table start to move when volume, operators, and calendar pressure show up together.

What usually breaks is not one dramatic failure. It is a stack of small drifts: a tolerance that was never photographed, a finishing step that only the sample maker knew how to baby, a packaging fit that worked at ten units and binds at two thousand.

Before the line starts, write down what “good” looks like in the factory’s language — measurements, reject examples, and a clear owner for borderline calls. That document becomes the shared reference at 2 a.m., when nobody wants to reopen a Figma file.

A first production run is less about inventing the product again and more about protecting the decisions you already made. Treat the sample as a promise, then audit whether the line can keep it under real speed.
